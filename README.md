## Overview

Stonehenge is a Neolithic and Bronze Age megalithic monument standing on Salisbury Plain about 2 miles (3 km) west of Amesbury, Wiltshire, England. The visible monument consists of a circular outer ring of vertical sarsen standing stones (each ~ 4 m / 13 ft tall), each pair capped by a horizontal lintel, with an inner horseshoe of sarsen trilithons and arrangements of smaller bluestones. Construction unfolded across at least three major stages between approximately 3100 BC and 1600 BC. The bluestones, weighing up to 4 tonnes each, were transported approximately 240 km from the Preseli Hills in Pembrokeshire, southwest Wales — a logistical feat unmatched among contemporary monuments. The larger sarsens are now traced by 2020 isotopic analysis to West Woods near Marlborough, ~ 25 km north. Stonehenge has attracted major archaeological investigation since William Stukeley's 18th-century surveys; modern interpretations include astronomical alignment (notably the summer-solstice sunrise and winter-solstice sunset axis), funerary use (Mike Parker Pearson's Stonehenge Riverside Project found extensive cremation burials), and use as a healing site (Geoffrey Wainwright and Timothy Darvill, drawing on the Welsh bluestone provenance). It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986. In folklore, Stonehenge is connected to Merlin (Geoffrey of Monmouth, c. 1136), the Devil, and the druids; modern druids and pagans use the site for solstice and equinox observances.

- **Origin**: Old English; first attested as 'Stanenges' or 'Stanheng' in 11th-12th century sources.
- **Meaning**: Generally interpreted as 'hanging stones' (stan = stone; henge from hengen, 'hanging' or 'gibbet'), a reference to the lintels suspended above the standing stones.
- **First use**: 11th–12th century, in Old English and Anglo-Latin sources

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Earth-energy / ley-line theory** _(John Michell, Paul Devereux)_

Late-20th-century earth-mysteries writers, building on Alfred Watkins's 1925 'ley lines', argue that Stonehenge sits at a node of natural energetic currents and was built to focus them.

**Druidic temple** _(William Stukeley, Modern druidic orders)_

William Stukeley's 18th-century claim that Stonehenge is a temple of the British druids; foundational to modern druidic practice at the site though now archaeologically untenable as a construction story (the Iron Age druids post-date construction by 2,000+ years).

### Scientific

**Astronomical observatory and ceremonial alignment** _(Norman Lockyer, Gerald Hawkins, Alexander Thom)_

The principal axis aligns with the midsummer sunrise (over the Heel Stone) and midwinter sunset, suggesting solstitial ceremony and possibly broader astronomical observation.

**Funerary monument and ancestor cult** _(Mike Parker Pearson)_

Stonehenge functioned principally as a cremation cemetery and ancestor monument, paired with the timber-built Durrington Walls as a complementary 'land of the living'.

**Healing site** _(Timothy Darvill, Geoffrey Wainwright)_

Bluestones, with their Welsh provenance and folkloric reputation, were transported as healing stones; injury patterns in some buried individuals are cited as supportive.

### Skeptical

**No singular function** _(Joshua Pollard, Aaron Watson)_

Stonehenge was used and reused over 1,500 years for purposes that shifted across phases — the search for a single 'meaning' is anachronistic.
